# REGULATION (EU) 2024/1689 - Artificial Intelligence Act (AI Act)

## REGULATION (EU) 2024/1689 OF THE EUROPEAN PARLIAMENT AND OF THE COUNCIL

of 13 June 2024

laying down harmonised rules on artificial intelligence and amending Regulations (EC) No 300/2008, (EU) No 167/2013, (EU) No 168/2013, (EU) 2018/858, (EU) 2018/1139 and (EU) 2019/2144 and Directives 2014/90/EU, (EU) 2016/797 and (EU) 2020/1828 (Artificial Intelligence Act)

(Text with EEA relevance)

---

## PREAMBLE (KEY RECITALS)

### Recital 1 - Purpose
The purpose of this Regulation is to improve the functioning of the internal market by laying down a uniform legal framework in particular for the development, the placing on the market, the putting into service and the use of artificial intelligence systems (AI systems) in the Union, in accordance with Union values, to promote the uptake of human centric and trustworthy artificial intelligence (AI) while ensuring a high level of protection of health, safety, fundamental rights as enshrined in the Charter of Fundamental Rights of the European Union (the 'Charter'), including democracy, the rule of law and environmental protection, to protect against the harmful effects of AI systems in the Union, and to support innovation.

### Recital 2 - Union Values
This Regulation should be applied in accordance with the values of the Union enshrined as in the Charter, facilitating the protection of natural persons, undertakings, democracy, the rule of law and environmental protection, while boosting innovation and employment and making the Union a leader in the uptake of trustworthy AI.

### Recital 4-5 - Benefits and Risks of AI
AI is a fast evolving family of technologies that contributes to a wide array of economic, environmental and societal benefits across the entire spectrum of industries and social activities. By improving prediction, optimising operations and resource allocation, and personalising digital solutions available for individuals and organisations, the use of AI can provide key competitive advantages to undertakings and support socially and environmentally beneficial outcomes.

At the same time, depending on the circumstances regarding its specific application, use, and level of technological development, AI may generate risks and cause harm to public interests and fundamental rights that are protected by Union law. Such harm might be material or immaterial, including physical, psychological, societal or economic harm.

### Recital 6 - Human-Centric AI
Given the major impact that AI can have on society and the need to build trust, it is vital for AI and its regulatory framework to be developed in accordance with Union values. As a prerequisite, AI should be a human-centric technology. It should serve as a tool for people, with the ultimate aim of increasing human well-being.

### Recital 12 - Definition of AI System
The notion of 'AI system' in this Regulation should be clearly defined and should be closely aligned with the work of international organisations working on AI to ensure legal certainty, facilitate international convergence and wide acceptance, while providing the flexibility to accommodate the rapid technological developments in this field. A key characteristic of AI systems is their capability to infer. This capability to infer refers to the process of obtaining the outputs, such as predictions, content, recommendations, or decisions, which can influence physical and virtual environments.

## KEY PROVISIONS SUMMARY

### Scope (Article 2)
- Applies to providers placing on the market or putting into service AI systems in the EU
- Applies to deployers of AI systems located within the EU
- Applies to providers and deployers located outside EU when AI output is used in EU
- Excludes AI systems used exclusively for military, defence, or national security purposes
- Excludes AI systems used solely for scientific research and development

### Risk-Based Classification

#### Prohibited AI Practices (Article 5)
The following AI practices are prohibited:
1. **Subliminal manipulation**: AI systems using subliminal techniques to materially distort behavior causing significant harm
2. **Exploitation of vulnerabilities**: Exploiting vulnerabilities of specific groups (age, disability, social/economic situation)
3. **Social scoring**: AI systems evaluating or classifying persons based on social behavior leading to detrimental treatment
4. **Real-time remote biometric identification in public spaces** (with exceptions for law enforcement)
5. **Emotion recognition in workplace and education** (with limited exceptions)
6. **Biometric categorization** based on sensitive attributes (race, political opinions, religious beliefs, sexual orientation)
7. **Untargeted scraping of facial images** from internet or CCTV for facial recognition databases
8. **Predictive policing** based solely on profiling

#### High-Risk AI Systems (Article 6 & Annex III)
High-risk AI systems include those used in:
- Biometric identification and categorisation
- Critical infrastructure management
- Education and vocational training
- Employment, workers management, and access to self-employment
- Access to essential services (credit scoring, emergency services)
- Law enforcement
- Migration, asylum, and border control management
- Administration of justice and democratic processes

#### Transparency Requirements (Article 50)
Providers must ensure that:
- AI systems intended to interact with natural persons are designed to inform persons they are interacting with AI
- Synthetic audio, image, video or text content must be marked as artificially generated or manipulated (deep fakes)
- Emotion recognition and biometric categorization systems inform persons of their operation

### Obligations for High-Risk AI Systems

#### For Providers (Articles 8-25):
- Risk management system
- Data governance and management
- Technical documentation
- Record-keeping (logging)
- Transparency and provision of information to deployers
- Human oversight measures
- Accuracy, robustness, and cybersecurity
- Quality management system
- CE marking
- Registration in EU database

#### For Deployers (Articles 26-29):
- Use AI systems in accordance with instructions
- Ensure human oversight
- Monitor operation and inform providers of serious incidents
- Conduct fundamental rights impact assessment (for public bodies and certain services)
- Inform natural persons when subject to high-risk AI decision-making

### General-Purpose AI Models (Articles 51-56)
Providers of general-purpose AI models must:
- Draw up and keep up-to-date technical documentation
- Provide information and documentation to downstream providers
- Put in place a policy to comply with copyright law
- Publish a summary of training data content

### Governance Structure
- **AI Office**: European Commission body for AI governance
- **European AI Board**: Advisory body with Member State representatives
- **National Competent Authorities**: Designated by each Member State
- **Market Surveillance Authorities**: Enforce the Regulation

### Penalties (Article 99)
- **Prohibited AI practices**: Up to €35 million or 7% of worldwide annual turnover
- **Non-compliance with other provisions**: Up to €15 million or 3% of turnover
- **Incorrect information to authorities**: Up to €7.5 million or 1% of turnover

### Timeline
- Entry into force: 1 August 2024
- Prohibited practices: Apply from 2 February 2025
- General-purpose AI provisions: Apply from 2 August 2025
- High-risk systems obligations: Apply from 2 August 2026
